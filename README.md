## FAQ
**Q: Why is your username "DairyProducts"?**

A: I played soccer as a kid (U11 and later U13), and one of my teammates called me "Dairy" and "Dairy Products" because he thought it sounded like my name, Derek. I have since used it as my username for basically everything online, so I'm stuck with this nickname now. I don't mind you calling me "Dairy" in real life, though, if you think it's funny!

**Q: Why did you choose computer engineering at UCSD?**

Serious A: They gave me a really nice scholarship, and I liked the program's flexibility...and I just really liked the campus and atmosphere (which might not be a popular opinion). Even though my interests are more software-oriented, I wanted to keep my options open and have the opportunity to learn about hardware and low-level systems if I wanted to. Most accredited CE/ECE programs are very rigid and strict with their requirements (that's why they have "threads," "tracks," or "concentrations") and force your hand into learning more about hardware than software. Many of these schools also separate CS and CE/EE/ECE departments, which (sometimes) can cause a bureaucratic red-tape nightmare if you want to take classes in the other department. UCSD's CE program is a lot more flexible, though, and lets you span your technical electives jointly across both the CSE and ECE departments, along with a pretty even split between software and hardware core requirements. A side effect of this is that the CE program at UCSD is not ABET-accredited; a lot of schools leave their CS/CE-adjacent programs unaccredited simply to avoid the aforementioned rigidity that comes with accreditation requirements. Notable examples besides UCSD include Stanford, UC Berkeley, and CMU.

**Q: Why did you get into CS/CE in general?**

A: To be honest, I was always interested in computers and technology, and I loved messing around with them since I was little. I got a programming-for-kids book from the library and found that I really, really liked building things with code, especially creating things that I could share with others and that would be of benefit/use/help to them. I would always imagine users and how they would interact with and feel about the things that I built, even when I was just starting out and when the stuff I made never left the home computer. This was before I ever found out about Silicon Valley, the tech industry, or SWE jobs and their salaries or anything like that. I know how stereotypical it is for a Bay Area guy to be into software, tech, and programming, and I'm sorry, but I genuinely just like it! No, my friends and family did not pressure me into it. No, I did not submit to the herd mentality of my peers and wider environment. I just like it purely of my own volition. Sorry!
